When to Sell an Underperforming Investment Property

3 min read
JSC Property Investments

Selling a poor investment feels like admitting a mistake, so many investors hold on far longer than they should. Others sell a good property at the wrong time because the last year felt slow. The decision deserves the same discipline as the purchase.

How do you know a property is underperforming?

Start with comparison, not feeling. Benchmarking performance against market conditions is essential (Yardney, 2025). Measure your property against:

  • Comparable properties in the same suburb. If similar homes have grown and yours has not, the issue is the asset.
  • The wider market. If the whole city is flat, your property may simply be at a different point in the cycle.
  • Your original plan. A property bought for cash flow and one bought for growth should be judged differently.

Warning signs include ongoing cash-flow strain beyond what you can comfortably manage, weak local fundamentals such as reliance on a single industry, and recurring maintenance, structural or strata problems (API Magazine, n.d.).

An underperforming property is not automatically a bad investment, because market cycles cause temporary slowdowns (API Magazine, n.d.).

Is it the asset or the cycle?

This is the central question.

A cycle problem looks like a property that is fundamentally sound, in a location with demand and scarce land, that has simply had a flat few years. Selling into a soft market can lock in a weak price.

An asset problem looks like something that cannot be fixed by time: an oversupplied unit block, a poor position on a busy road, a building with defects, or a location with declining demand. Time rarely fixes these.

What does it cost to switch properties?

More than most investors estimate. On an $800,000 Queensland property:

  • Selling: Queensland agent commission averages 2.48% including GST, and total selling costs come to about $22,400 to $22,600, excluding marketing and any auctioneer fee (Webb, 2026).
  • Buying again: Transfer duty on an $800,000 investment purchase is about $29,000 (Queensland Revenue Office [QRO], 2026).

That is roughly $51,500 before marketing, capital gains tax, loan costs and the new property’s own purchase costs. These are our own calculations from the sources above.

How do you work out if switching pays?

Compare the switching cost with the extra growth or income you expect from the replacement. As a rough illustration, if a replacement $800,000 property grows two percentage points a year faster than your current one, that is about $16,000 extra a year. It would take more than three years to recover the $51,500 switching cost, before tax. This is our own illustration, not a forecast.

If you cannot point to a clear, lasting performance gap, switching may not pay.

How do the 2026 and 2027 tax changes affect selling?

You may lose negative gearing on the replacement. If you own an established property bought before 12 May 2026, it keeps its existing negative gearing treatment until you sell (Acumentis, 2026). Replace it with another established property and, from 1 July 2027, that new property no longer qualifies for negative gearing (Baker McKenzie, 2026). New builds remain exempt.

There is no need to rush a sale before 1 July 2027 for CGT reasons alone. Gains accrued before that date keep the current rules, including the 50% discount where eligible, even if you sell later (ATO, 2026). What matters is having evidence of the property’s value at that date.

Later growth is taxed differently. Gains accruing after 1 July 2027 use cost base indexation and a 30% minimum tax (ATO, 2026). Model both scenarios with your accountant.

When holding is the right call

Sometimes the right thing to do is nothing (Yardney, 2025). If the property is fundamentally sound, the cash flow is manageable and the market is soft, holding may beat selling at a discount. Improving the property, refinancing or adjusting rent may fix a performance problem more cheaply than selling.

Frequently asked questions

When should I sell an investment property? When the property has an asset problem time will not fix, and the switching costs are clearly outweighed by a better replacement.

How much does it cost to sell a property in Queensland? Agent commission averages about 2.48% including GST, plus conveyancing, marketing and loan discharge costs.
