Depreciation Schedules: What a Quantity Surveyor Report Is Worth on New Builds vs Older Stock

4 min read
JSC Property Investments

A depreciation schedule is one of the few investment property costs that can pay for itself many times over. On a new build it can be worth tens of thousands of dollars in deductions in the first few years. On a 1970s house, it may be worth very little. Knowing which you are buying matters before you order one, and before you choose the property.

What is a depreciation schedule?

It is a report, usually prepared by a quantity surveyor, that sets out the tax deductions you can claim for the decline in value of an investment property. It covers two types of claim.

Capital works (the building). For residential construction that started on or after 16 September 1987, you can claim 2.5% of the construction cost each year over 40 years (Australian Taxation Office [ATO], 2026a). If the actual construction cost is unknown, a quantity surveyor or other qualified person can estimate it. You cannot use the purchase price, insured cost or replacement cost instead (ATO, 2026a).

Plant and equipment (the fittings). Items such as appliances, carpets and blinds that wear out faster than the building.

The fee for the estimate is deductible in the year you pay it (ATO, 2026a).

Why the gap between new and old is so wide

Since 7:30pm on 9 May 2017, most individual investors cannot claim depreciation on second-hand plant and equipment in a residential rental property (ATO, 2026b). That removed a large share of the claim on established homes, while new builds kept it.

The numbers show the result. Research by the Property Investment Professionals of Australia and MCG Quantity Surveyors found average first-year deductions of:

  • Houses: $20,723 new, compared with $6,281 established
  • Townhouses: $18,957 new, compared with $8,125 established
  • Units: $23,124 new, compared with $6,526 established (Crotty, 2026).

What can you still claim on older stock?

More than many investors assume.

  • The building claim continues. Losing the plant and equipment claim on a second-hand property does not affect the capital works claim (Farrugia, 2026). A home built in 2000 still has capital works deductions until 2040.
  • Renovations count. Even if a house was built before 1987, renovations carried out after 15 September 1987, including by previous owners, may still qualify for capital works deductions (BMT Tax Depreciation, n.d.). A quantity surveyor can identify these.
  • New items you install can be depreciated. If you buy a new oven or air-conditioner for the property, its decline in value is claimable.

When is a depreciation schedule not worth it?

A schedule usually earns its fee when the building was constructed after 1987, has had significant renovations, or is new. It may not when the property is an older home with no qualifying renovations, or when the expected claims are smaller than the fee. A quantity surveyor can usually give you an indication before you commit.

How do the 2026 tax reforms change the picture?

New builds keep both advantages. New builds keep full negative gearing under the reforms and also keep the full plant and equipment claim. As Farrugia (2026) puts it, the two now “sit on the same side of the ledger”.

Deductions on post-budget established properties may be quarantined. For established residential property bought after the budget cut-off, losses can only be used against residential property income (Farrugia, 2026). A depreciation-driven loss still has value, but it may be deferred rather than offset against your wages each year.

Depreciation affects your eventual capital gain. Capital works deductions you claim must be taken into account when working out your capital gain or loss (ATO, 2026a). Ask your accountant how this interacts with the new CGT rules from 1 July 2027.

Depreciation should not drive the purchase

The deduction is real, but it is a tax benefit on a cost, not a return. As PIPA chair Cate Bakos says: “Depreciation enhances a strong asset, but it doesn’t rescue a weak one” (Crotty, 2026). Land content, location and rental demand still decide how a property performs.

Frequently asked questions

Is a quantity surveyor report tax deductible? Yes. The ATO allows a deduction for fees paid for a capital works estimate in the year you pay them.

Can I claim depreciation on an established property? Yes, on the building if it was constructed after 15 September 1987, on qualifying renovations, and on new items you install. Most second-hand fittings are not claimable.

This article is general information only and is not tax advice. Speak to a registered tax agent and a qualified quantity surveyor about your property.
