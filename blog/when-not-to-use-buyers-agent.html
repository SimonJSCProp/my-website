When Does It Make Sense Not to Use a Buyer's Agent?

4 min read
JSC Property Investments

A buyer's agent adds real value for many buyers, but not for everyone, and it is worth being honest about when you genuinely do not need one. Paying for representation you will not use is just a cost. Here are the situations where going without a buyer's agent makes sense:

When you know the market cold

The strongest reason to skip a buyer's agent is genuine local market expertise. If you have bought and sold in an area before, you know its suburbs and streets well, and you can read comparable sales and judge fair value with confidence, you are doing much of what a buyer's agent does. Someone buying their third property in a suburb they have lived in for a decade often knows that market better than an agent parachuting in. If you can reliably tell a good buy from a bad one and price it correctly, you are paying for less than you think.

When you can negotiate and stay objective

A large part of a buyer's agent's value is negotiation and emotional distance. If you are comfortable negotiating against an experienced selling agent, can hold your position, and can walk away from a property you have fallen for rather than overpaying, you have the temperament the job requires. Plenty of buyers do not, which is the real argument for representation, but if you do, and you are confident bidding at auction or negotiating a private sale, you can run the process yourself.

When you have the time to do it properly

Buying well is time-consuming: researching suburbs, monitoring listings, inspecting, doing due diligence and negotiating all take hours that many people do not have, which is why time-poor professionals value a buyer's agent. But if you have the time and are willing to put it in, you can do the legwork yourself. The work is not mysterious; it is just demanding. A buyer with the hours to research thoroughly, attend inspections and chase down comparable sales can substitute their own effort for the fee.

When the purchase is straightforward

The simpler the purchase, the less an agent adds. Buying a standard, well-understood property in a familiar market, with no complex due diligence and no fierce competition, is a transaction many capable buyers can handle alone. The value of a buyer's agent rises with complexity: an interstate purchase, an unfamiliar market, a competitive or opaque buying environment, or a property with complications. Remove those, and the case for paying for help weakens.

When the numbers genuinely do not work

A buyer's agent has to earn their fee, and sometimes the maths is tight. On a lower-priced property, a fee of several thousand dollars or a percentage of the price is a meaningful share of the budget, and if you are confident, you can buy well without help, that money may be better kept. The fee is justified when the agent saves you more than they cost in price, time and avoided mistakes; if you have good reason to believe you can capture those savings yourself, the spend is harder to justify.

Be honest about which buyer you are

The risk is overestimating your own ability. Many buyers believe they know a market and can negotiate, then overpay or miss out because they were emotionally attached or out-negotiated by a professional who does this daily. The honest test is whether you can genuinely value a property, stay objective, and negotiate hard, not whether you would like to think you can. If you can, and the purchase is straightforward in a market you know, you may not need a buyer's agent. If you cannot, or you are buying somewhere unfamiliar or against strong competition, that is precisely when representation pays for itself. The decision should rest on a clear-eyed read of your own skills and circumstances, not on wanting to save the fee.
