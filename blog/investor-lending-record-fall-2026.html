Investor Lending Just Recorded Its Biggest Fall Ever: What It Means for Buyers

4 min read
JSC Property Investments

A year ago, investors were taking out more home loans than at any time on record. In the September quarter 2026, the number of new investor loans fell 30.6% to 35,301, the largest quarterly fall since the series began in 2002. The previous record was not just beaten. The fall was almost double it (Australian Bureau of Statistics [ABS], 2026).

What does the data show?

The value of new investor lending fell 32.3% to $24.7 billion. It was the third straight quarterly decline, with strong falls in every state and territory. Annual growth in investor loan numbers swung from 24.9% in the December quarter 2025 to minus 38.5% (ABS, 2026).

Every type of borrower pulled back. Owner-occupier loans fell 5.3%, led by Queensland at minus 10.4%. First home buyer loans fell 8.6%, although first home buyers still make up more than a third of owner-occupier lending (ABS, 2026). Total new lending fell 15.5% in value, also a record.

Why did investor lending fall so far?

The ABS says the causes go beyond interest rates. “Our data suggests that investor activity is being influenced by factors beyond increasing borrowing costs, including the potential impacts of tax changes and expectations of lower house prices,” said Dr Mish Tan, ABS head of finance statistics (ABS, 2026).

Three forces arrived at once:

  • Tax changes. Negative gearing and CGT reforms announced in the May Budget, plus the ban on SMSF limited recourse borrowing.
  • Five rate rises in 2026. The data predates the November increase (Martin, 2026).
  • Falling prices. National values fell for a seventh straight month in October (Cuthbert, 2026), and buyers rarely rush into a falling market.

Timing may also have flattered the June quarter and exaggerated the September fall. Investor settlements often take 60 to 90 days, so some September-quarter loans were for pre-Budget purchases, and there was a rush of SMSF purchases before a 10 August deadline (Martin, 2026).

Where did the fall hit hardest?

Established homes. Investor loans to buy existing dwellings fell 35.6% to 25,843, accounting for about 92% of the total fall (The Good Builder, 2026). By value, investor lending for existing homes fell 38% in the quarter and 46% over the year (Gordon, 2026).

New builds held up better but did not grow. Construction and newly built purchases rose to a record 27.6% share of investor loans, but only because everything else shrank. Investor new-build loans themselves fell 8.7% (The Good Builder, 2026).

What does it mean for buyers?

Less competition for established homes. The market investors have retreated from is exactly where many owner-occupiers want to buy. Fewer investor bidders means more room to negotiate on price and conditions.

Do not assume first home buyers will fill the gap. “Even first home buyers, who these changes were meant to help, are hesitant to purchase right now,” said Herron Todd White chief economist Cameron Kusher (Martin, 2026).

Borrowing power is the real constraint. Lendi Group modelling shows a couple earning $181,000 has lost almost $103,000 in borrowing power across 2026’s five rate rises (Martin, 2026). Falling prices help less when the bank lends less.

What could it mean for renters and supply?

Investors fund much of Australia’s new housing. The Property Council says investors make up about half of buyers in new apartment projects, which need pre-sales to secure construction finance (Saunders, 2026). Master Builders Australia warned: “We only think this is the start. It’s going to get worse” (Saunders, 2026).

Fewer new rental homes, combined with fewer investors buying established stock, could keep pressure on rents. That cuts both ways: harder for tenants, and potentially supportive for investors who do buy.

What should buyers do now?

  • Owner-occupiers: Use the softer competition to negotiate, but buy within your limits at today’s rates.
  • Investors: Fewer competitors can mean better buying, but run cash flow without negative gearing on post-Budget established purchases.
  • Everyone: Watch the next quarterly lending figures to see whether investors have found a floor.

Frequently asked questions

How much did investor lending fall in 2026? New investor loans fell 30.6% in the September quarter 2026 and were 38.5% lower than a year earlier.

Why are property investors pulling back? Higher interest rates, the 2026 tax changes and expectations of lower house prices, according to the ABS.
