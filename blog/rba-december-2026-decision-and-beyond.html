The RBA’s December Decision (and Beyond): What to Watch Before 8 December and Into 2027

4 min read
JSC Property Investments

The Reserve Bank’s last decision of 2026 lands at 2:30pm on Tuesday 8 December (Aussie, 2026). Since the November rise to 4.85%, the data has pulled in two directions at once. Inflation went up. So did unemployment. That is the tension the Board has to resolve, and it matters for anyone planning to buy in early 2027. December is not the end of the story either: the data released over summer will shape the first decision of 2027.

Where do things stand?

The cash rate is 4.85% after five rises in 2026. November’s decision split the Board six votes to three (Aussie, 2026).

The minutes, released on 17 November, show why. The majority believed the inflation outlook had deteriorated and that financial conditions might not be restrictive enough. The three dissenters pointed to the delayed effect of earlier rises, a larger-than-expected rise in unemployment and the housing downturn, and worried the economy could be approaching a “materially sharper downturn” (Commonwealth Bank of Australia [CBA], 2026).

What has the data shown since November?

Unemployment jumped. The unemployment rate rose to 4.9% in October, its highest since October 2021, with 44,000 fewer people employed (Australian Bureau of Statistics [ABS], 2026c).

Wages picked up. The Wage Price Index rose 1.1% in the September quarter, the strongest quarterly growth since December 2023, and 3.5% over the year (ABS, 2026b).

Inflation rose again. Annual CPI inflation reached 4.9% in October, up from 4.3% in September. Trimmed mean inflation, the RBA’s preferred underlying measure, rose from 3.6% to 3.8% (ABS, 2026a). Housing was the largest contributor, up 6.4%, with electricity up 25.2% after Commonwealth rebates ended (Ell, 2026).

Oxford Economics’ Harry Murphy Cruise summed it up: “Rising inflation and unemployment are the RBA’s worst nightmare” (Shteyman, 2026).

What data is still to come?

The September quarter national accounts, including GDP, are due on 2 December (ABS, n.d.). A weak result would strengthen the case of the three members who voted to hold in November.

What do economists expect?

Most expect a hold.

  • CBA: “We judge December will not be a live meeting after two back-to-back interest rate hikes,” said head of Australian economics Belinda Allen. She added that February “does remain ‘live’” (CBA, 2026).
  • Oxford Economics: Expects a hold in December and the cash rate to stay at 4.85% throughout 2027 (Shteyman, 2026).
  • ANZ: Has brought its forecast for a first rate cut forward to June 2027 (Shteyman, 2026).

The forecasts disagree on what comes next, and that disagreement is itself the signal. The next move in 2027 is genuinely uncertain.

What to watch on 8 December

  • The decision. A hold is the most likely outcome. A rise would be a surprise.
  • The vote. Whether the split widens, narrows or disappears tells you which way the Board is leaning.
  • The statement. Look for whether the Board keeps describing risks to inflation as tilted to the upside.
  • The Governor’s media conference. Comments about February and about the labour market will move expectations.

What comes after 8 December?

The December decision sets the tone, but the next six weeks of data will decide February. These are the dates to mark:

  • 17 December 2026: Labour force figures for November (ABS, 2026d). Another rise in unemployment would strengthen the case for holding.
  • 6 January 2027: Monthly CPI for November (ABS, 2026a). This shows whether October was the peak, as NAB economists expected (Shteyman, 2026).
  • 21 January 2027: Labour force figures for December (ABS, 2026d).
  • 27 January 2027: CPI for December, including the full December quarter (ABS, 2026a). This is the most important release before February.
  • 9 February 2027: The first RBA decision of 2027, which industry calendars list for 9 February (Vasquez, 2026).

How to read the outcome

If the RBA holds in December, watch whether the statement still leans towards further increases. A hold with a hawkish tone keeps February live. A hold with a softer tone would suggest the cycle has peaked.

If the RBA raises in December, that would signal the majority now sees inflation as the bigger risk than unemployment. Expect lenders to pass it on within days and borrowing capacity to fall again.

Either way, the January CPI release on 27 January is likely to matter more for February than anything said in December.

What does it mean for buyers?

A hold is not relief. The cash rate stays at its highest level since 2008, and borrowing capacity does not recover.

Do not plan around cuts. Even the most optimistic major bank forecast puts the first cut in mid-2027. Budget at today’s rate and test at 5.10%.

The quiet period can favour prepared buyers. December and January are traditionally slower months. With fewer investors active and values falling, a buyer with finance in place has leverage that may not last once rates turn.

Refresh your pre-approval in January. If you plan to buy in February or March, ask your broker to reassess your borrowing capacity after the 27 January CPI, before the February decision.

Frequently asked questions

When is the next RBA decision? Tuesday 8 December 2026 at 2:30pm AEDT, followed by the first decision of 2027, listed for 9 February.

Will the RBA raise rates in December 2026? Most economists expect a hold, though higher-than-expected inflation keeps February 2027 in play.

What data matters most before February 2027? The December quarter CPI on 27 January 2027, followed by the labour force figures for November and December.
