Directors' Summary

Directors' Summary

Redland City is a bayside lifestyle market on Brisbane's eastern doorstep, with Cleveland about 26 kilometres from the CBD. Its population reached 172,800 in June 2025 and is planned to grow to around 212,000 by 2046, steady growth in a city that is largely built out.

The appeal here is scarcity, not affordability. Bounded by Moreton Bay, protected green space and koala habitat, Redland has limited room to expand, and in 2024 it recorded the strongest land-price growth in South East Queensland. Houses sit in line with Brisbane at around $1.15 million, vacancy is tight at about 1.1%, and demand is led by owner-occupiers and downsizers rather than speculation.

We buy established houses in proven Redland pockets, on the data, and steer clear of flood-prone and habitat-constrained land. For investors chasing yield, units and townhouses offer a lower entry point; for long-run capital growth, the bayside scarcity story is the draw, with a confirmed 2032 Olympic venue at Birkdale adding to the case. No developer stock, no kickbacks.

Location Map

Redland City sits on the bay, about 26 km east of the Brisbane CBD, a bayside city in South East Queensland. Cleveland is about 26 km from the CBD and Redland Bay about 38 km, with the Gold Coast less than 70 km away and the Sunshine Coast around 120 km. The Cleveland rail line runs to Brisbane, with bus and ferry links across the bay, and a confirmed Brisbane 2032 venue at Birkdale will host Olympic and Paralympic canoe slalom. The city spreads along the southern Moreton Bay coast, including North Stradbroke Island (Minjerribah).

04

Future Prospects

Redland's future is shaped less by megaprojects than by limited land, steady council investment, a confirmed 2032 Olympic venue and the broader Brisbane uplift. The headline is the Redland Whitewater Centre, a confirmed Brisbane 2032 venue at the master-planned Birkdale Community Precinct, funded by the Australian and Queensland governments and owned long-term by Council. It will host Olympic canoe slalom and double as a swift-water rescue and tourism asset.

The Southern Thornlands Priority Development Area, declared in 2025 and led by Economic Development Queensland, is planned to help house the city's growth toward 212,000 by 2046, with community consultation through 2026. Toondah Harbour, a long-debated Cleveland waterfront precinct, is progressing as a scaled-back revised concept after the original 3,600-apartment plan was refused on environmental grounds, so it is one to watch rather than bank on.

Redland City's economy is worth around $8.95 billion, about 1.69% of Queensland's output. Unlike its industrial neighbours, Redland's rates base is largely residential, and the economy leans on health care, retail, education, construction and a marine and tourism sector built around the bay.

Tourism is a genuine driver: about 1.2 million visitors a year contribute roughly $399 million to the local economy. The population is ageing, with the over-65s the fastest-growing group, and most future growth is forecast to come from smaller households, singles and downsizers.

For property, the equation is different from the cheaper growth corridors. Supply is constrained, demand is owner-occupier led, and vacancy is tight. That scarcity, more than any single industry, is what supports prices and rents over the long run.

Established Houses, Proven Pockets

We actively target around 20% of the suburbs in Redland City. Not every suburb makes the cut, and within the ones that do, only certain pockets and streets do.

Our focus is established detached houses in proven, well-connected suburbs, close to infrastructure, jobs and amenity. We avoid flood-prone areas and oversupplied fringe estates. Land matters: the dwelling depreciates, the land appreciates, so position and land content drive long-term growth.

Most quality investment-grade houses in our target pockets now sit between $1,000,000 and $1,300,000 or more, with entry-level opportunities from around $900,000. Closer to the city, well-located houses on 450 m² stack up; further out we prefer 600 m² and above. Gross yields run around 2.8% on houses, higher on units and townhouses.
